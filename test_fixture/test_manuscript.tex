\title{Curvature and salt dependence in disordered filament networks}

\section{Introduction}
Intermediate filament networks and neurofilament assemblies govern the mechanical
response of the axon. Disordered protein tails mediate interactions between
neighbouring filaments, and the resulting network stiffness depends strongly on
ionic conditions. Vimentin and neurofilament proteins show related but distinct
behaviour under compression.

\section{Methods}
Small-angle X-ray scattering was used to determine the interfilament spacing and the
network structure as a function of salt concentration. Osmotic stress measurements
provided the force-distance relationship. Entropy of the disordered tail ensemble was
estimated from compression of simulated configurations.

\section{Results and Discussion}
Myelin membrane structure changes with lipid composition, and the transition is
relevant to demyelinating disease. Self-assembly of aromatic peptides into rigid
supramolecular materials offers a synthetic comparison for the tail-mediated
interactions we observe.
